Build an AI Study Assistant for Android

A good AI study assistant doesn't just hand out answers. It explains, quizzes and grounds itself in what the learner is actually studying. This project combines retrieval over notes, structured quiz generation, chat, and spaced-repetition flashcards.

Learners import notes or chapters, then ask questions, get explanations grounded in their material, and practise with generated quizzes and flashcards. The design goal is understanding, not shortcuts.

Features and architecture

1. Ask with grounding (RAG)

Retrieve the most relevant chunks from the learner's notes, then answer using only them, with citations. See RAG on Android for indexing and retrieval details.

2. Generate quizzes as structured data

Validate every question: model output can have duplicate options or out-of-range answers.

3. Flashcards with spaced repetition

This is a simplified SM-2-style scheduler. Learners can edit generated cards before saving them.

Design for learning, not copying

  • Offer Hint mode before full answers.
  • End explanations with a check question.
  • Show citations to the learner's notes, so they can verify.
  • Track quiz results to suggest weak topics.
  • Be clear that AI can be wrong. See Responsible AI UX.

Safety and privacy

  • If your audience includes minors, follow applicable regulations and platform policies (for example Google Play's Families policies).
  • Imported notes may be sent to a cloud model: disclose this and minimise what's sent (only retrieved chunks).
  • Treat imported content as untrusted: it may contain instructions aimed at the model. See Prompt Injection.

Evaluate quality

Create a small test set of questions with known answers from sample notes, and check groundedness, correctness and refusal when information is missing. See Evaluating AI Responses.

Frequently asked questions

Can it work offline?

Flashcard review works offline. Q&A and generation need a model: cloud (online) or on-device (limited capability on supported devices).

Why not just use a general chatbot?

Grounding in the learner's material, quizzes and spaced repetition make it a learning tool rather than an answer machine.

Conclusion

A study assistant grounds answers in the learner's notes with citations, generates validated quizzes and flashcards with spaced repetition, and is designed to encourage understanding, with clear privacy and safety choices.
