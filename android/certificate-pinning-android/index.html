Certificate Pinning: Benefits and Limitations

Certificate pinning makes your app trust only specific keys for your servers, which blocks some man-in-the-middle attacks. Done carelessly, it can also lock every user out of your app when a certificate rotates. Here's when pinning is worth it and how to do it safely.

TLS already authenticates your server through trusted certificate authorities (CAs). Pinning adds a stricter rule: even a valid certificate from a trusted CA is rejected unless its public key matches one you've pinned. That protects against mis-issued certificates and some interception setups, at the cost of operational risk.

What pinning does and doesn't protect

Android apps targeting API 24+ already ignore user-installed CAs by default, which blocks many casual interception setups. Pinning additionally defends against mis-issued certificates from trusted CAs. It does not stop an attacker who controls the device and modifies your app.

Pinning safely

  1. Pin public keys, not certificates, and preferably an intermediate CA or your own key that you control across renewals.
  2. Always include backup pins: a second key you hold offline, or another CA you might move to.
  3. Set an expiration date so very old app versions don't break permanently.
  4. Coordinate with whoever manages certificates, such as the backend team or CDN, and document the rotation process.
  5. Monitor pinning failures and have a plan, such as a forced update, if something goes wrong.

Should you pin?

Consider pinning

  • Banking, payments, high-value targets
  • You control the certificate lifecycle
  • Strong operational processes

Probably don't

  • Third-party APIs or CDNs you don't control
  • No process for coordinated key rotation
  • Small teams where an outage risk outweighs the benefit

For many apps, correct TLS, the default distrust of user CAs, server-side validation and Play Integrity provide better risk reduction per unit of operational effort.

Testing

  • Verify the pin values match the production chain before release, using a staging environment that mirrors production certificates.
  • Test that a deliberately wrong pin fails closed, and that debug overrides work for development proxies.

Frequently asked questions

Does pinning stop someone reverse engineering my API?

Not reliably. An attacker controlling their own device can modify the app to remove pinning. Pinning protects honest users' connections, not your API from its own clients.

What happens when my certificate renews?

If the public key changes and isn't in your pin set, connections fail for every app version with the old pins. That's why key reuse, backup pins and expirations matter.

Is pinning required by Google Play?

No. It's an optional hardening measure.

Conclusion

Certificate pinning adds protection against mis-issued or unexpected certificates, but brings real outage risk. If you pin, pin public keys with backups and an expiration, coordinate rotations, and monitor. Otherwise, rely on solid TLS defaults and server-side controls.
