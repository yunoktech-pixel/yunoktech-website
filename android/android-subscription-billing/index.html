🚀 App Publishing, Monetization, and Product Development

Subscription Billing in Android Apps

Subscriptions turn an app into a business, and billing bugs turn paying users into angry ones. Google Play Billing handles payments, but entitlement, verification and lifecycle events are your job. Here's the architecture and the code that make subscriptions reliable.

The happy path, where a user taps "Subscribe", pays and gets access, is a small part of subscription billing. The rest is renewals, failed payments and grace periods, upgrades, refunds, restores on new devices and fraud. A server-backed design handles all of them consistently.

Keep the library current

Google deprecates older Play Billing Library versions on a published schedule. As of late 2026, new apps and updates can no longer use version 7 or older. Check the deprecation FAQ and use the latest version. API shapes change between major versions, so verify the code below against the reference docs for your version.

Architecture

Products, base plans and offers

  • A subscription product (for example premium) has one or more base plans (monthly, yearly; auto-renewing or prepaid).
  • Offers add trials or introductory prices to a base plan, with eligibility rules.
  • Create them in Play Console → Monetize. The app queries them at runtime, so never hard-code prices.

Client flow

Acknowledge within the deadline

Purchases not acknowledged within three days are refunded automatically. Acknowledge on the server after verification (or on the client with acknowledgePurchase if you have no backend).

Server responsibilities

  1. Verify each purchase token with the Google Play Developer API (subscriptions v2 endpoint).
  2. Link the purchase to your user account (and guard against token reuse across accounts).
  3. Acknowledge the purchase.
  4. Store the entitlement with its expiry and state.
  5. Process RTDN (real-time developer notifications via Pub/Sub) for renewals, grace periods, account holds, cancellations and refunds, re-querying the API for the latest state.

Subscription states to handle in UI

ActiveFullNormal premium experience
Cancelled (still in period)Until expiry"Your plan ends on …", resubscribe option
Grace periodUsually keep accessAsk to fix payment method
Account holdRemove accessExplain and link to Play subscription centre
Expired / refundedNoneOffer to resubscribe

Testing

  • Add license testers in Play Console, whose purchases use test payment methods.
  • Upload to a testing track: billing requires the app to be published on a track with matching signing.
  • Test renewals (accelerated for testers), cancellation, grace period and restore on a second device.

Examples

Content subscription

Monthly and yearly base plans, a 7-day trial offer, and server-checked entitlements for premium content.

Remove ads (one-time)

A one-time product, acknowledged and verified, with the entitlement cached locally and checked periodically.

Multi-platform account

Entitlements stored per user on the server, so access works across the user's devices.

Frequently asked questions

Can I skip the backend?

For simple one-time unlocks you can verify and acknowledge on the device, but it's easier to defraud and harder to sync across devices. Subscriptions benefit greatly from a backend.

How do users manage or cancel?

Through the Play Store subscription centre. Provide a deep link from your app's settings.

Should I use a subscription management service?

Third-party services can handle verification, RTDN and analytics for you. Evaluate their cost and data handling against building it yourself.

Conclusion

Reliable subscriptions need Play Billing on the client, verification, acknowledgement and entitlements on your server, RTDN for lifecycle events, clear UI for every state, and thorough testing with license testers. Keep the Billing Library up to date with Google's schedule.
