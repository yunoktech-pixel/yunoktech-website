Android Permissions and Privacy Best Practices

Every permission you request is a trust decision for the user, and a policy obligation for you. Modern Android offers privacy-preserving alternatives for many old permission requests. Here's how to request less, ask better, and stay compliant.

A camera app asking for contacts, a flashlight asking for location: users notice, and they uninstall. Google Play also restricts sensitive permissions and requires accurate data disclosures. Good permission design is both better UX and better compliance.

Principles

  1. Avoid the permission if a privacy-preserving API exists (Photo Picker, document picker, Credential Manager).
  2. Ask in context, when the user triggers the feature, not at app launch.
  3. Request the minimum: approximate instead of precise location, and foreground instead of background.
  4. Degrade gracefully when denied: the rest of the app must keep working.

Permission-free alternatives

NeedInstead ofUse
Pick photos/videosStorage or media read permissionsPhoto Picker (PickVisualMedia)
Open a documentBroad storage accessStorage Access Framework (OpenDocument)
Take one photoCAMERA in your own camera UI (if not needed)Camera intent (TakePicture)
Rough location (weather, city)ACCESS_FINE_LOCATIONACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION (approximate)
Sign-in with saved credentialsReading accountsCredential Manager

Sensitive and restricted permissions

Background location, SMS and call log, all-files access, exact alarms, accessibility services and others are restricted by Google Play policy and require declarations or justifications. Use them only when they're core to your app's functionality, and check current Play policy before building features around them.

Disclosure and Data safety

  • Your Play Console Data safety form must accurately describe data collection and sharing, including by SDKs.
  • Provide a privacy policy that matches actual behaviour.
  • Use prominent in-app disclosure before collecting sensitive data where Play policy requires it.
  • Audit third-party SDKs: their collection counts as yours.

Examples

Profile photo

The Photo Picker, with no permission at all.

Weather app

Approximate location, requested when the user taps "Use my location", with manual city entry as a fallback.

Delivery notifications

POST_NOTIFICATIONS requested after the first order, not on first launch.

Frequently asked questions

Can I request several permissions at once?

You can, with RequestMultiplePermissions, but users trust focused, contextual requests more. Bundle only those needed for one feature.

What happens if the user selects "Only this time"?

The permission is granted temporarily and revoked later. Always re-check before use.

Do unused apps lose permissions?

Yes. Android can auto-reset permissions for apps that haven't been used for a while, so always check at runtime.

Conclusion

Request fewer, smaller, contextual permissions, explain why before asking, prefer privacy-preserving APIs like the Photo Picker, handle denial gracefully, and keep your Data safety disclosures accurate.
