🔐 Android Security and Privacy

Protecting User Data in AI-Powered Android Apps

AI features are hungry for context: messages, photos, documents, location. Every byte you send to a model is data you're responsible for. This guide shows how to design AI features that are useful and respectful of user privacy, from architecture to disclosure.

A "summarise my chats" feature is helpful, and it also means private conversations may travel to a cloud model. Users deserve to know, to choose, and to trust that you sent only what was needed. Privacy-respecting AI is mostly about design decisions made early.

Principles

1. Minimise input

  • Send the specific text needed (one note), not the whole database.
  • Use on-device OCR or extraction first, and send structured fields rather than raw photos.
  • Strip image metadata (GPS, device info) before upload.
  • Downscale images and truncate long inputs. That's less data and lower cost.

2. Prefer on-device processing

Gemini Nano via ML Kit GenAI processes data on the device. Android's documentation states that AICore doesn't retain inputs or outputs after processing. For supported tasks and devices, that's the most private option. See Cloud vs On-Device AI.

3. Redact before cloud calls

Pattern redaction catches obvious identifiers. It won't catch names or context, so combine it with minimisation and consent rather than relying on it alone.

4. Consent and disclosure

  • Ask before the first cloud processing of personal content, and remember the choice in DataStore.
  • Make the setting reversible and visible.
  • Keep your privacy policy and Play Data safety declarations accurate, including data sent to AI providers.

5. Provider terms and configuration

  • Read the AI provider's data usage terms for your tier. Free and paid tiers may differ in how inputs can be used.
  • Prefer enterprise or paid tiers with clear data handling for production user data, where appropriate.
  • Choose regions and data residency options if regulations require them.

6. Retention and logging

  • Don't log raw prompts or responses by default. Log metadata such as tokens, latency, model and error codes.
  • If you store AI outputs (summaries, chats), treat them as user data: protect them, allow export and deletion, and apply retention limits.
  • Make account deletion remove AI-derived data too. See AI Privacy and Data Retention.

Examples

Chat summary

On-device where supported, otherwise opt-in cloud with redaction and no server-side storage.

Receipt scanning

On-device OCR, then send only the amounts and merchant text, with photos never uploaded.

Health journal insights

On-device only, or no AI. Some data shouldn't leave the device at all.

Frequently asked questions

Is sending data to an AI API "sharing" for Data safety?

It depends on the relationship (for example, a service provider processing on your behalf) and Play's definitions. Review the Data safety guidance carefully and disclose accurately.

Can users opt out of AI features entirely?

They should be able to. Provide a setting to disable cloud AI processing, and ideally all AI features.

Does redaction make cloud AI safe for any data?

No. Redaction reduces exposure of obvious identifiers. Highly sensitive categories may require on-device processing or no AI at all.

Conclusion

Privacy-respecting AI minimises data, prefers on-device processing, redacts before cloud calls, asks for informed consent, understands provider terms, and limits retention and logging, all reflected honestly in your disclosures.
