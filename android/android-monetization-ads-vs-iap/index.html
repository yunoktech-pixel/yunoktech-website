🚀 App Publishing, Monetization, and Product Development

Android App Monetization: Ads vs In-App Purchases

Ads monetise attention, and purchases monetise value. The right model depends on how often people use your app, what they'd pay for, and the experience you want to protect. Here's a practical comparison, including policy rules that limit your options.

Many first-time app makers add banner ads by default and earn very little while damaging the experience. Others put everything behind a paywall and get few installs. Monetisation works when the model matches how users get value from the app.

The main models

ModelEarns fromFitsWatch out for
Ads (banner, interstitial, rewarded, native)Impressions and clicks; revenue per user is usually smallHigh-frequency, broad-audience apps (utilities, casual games, content)UX damage, ad policy violations, privacy disclosures
One-time purchasesUnlocks (pro version, packs)Tools with clear premium featuresOne-off revenue; needs ongoing new users
SubscriptionsRecurring accessOngoing value: content, cloud sync, servicesMust deliver continuous value; churn
HybridAds for free users, plus a purchase to remove ads or unlock featuresMany consumer appsBalance so the free tier stays usable

Ads done right

  • Rewarded ads (the user chooses to watch for a benefit) are less intrusive and often perform well.
  • Native ads blend with content when clearly labelled.
  • Interstitials only at natural breaks, never on launch, during input or repeatedly.
  • Follow ad network and Play policies: no accidental-click layouts, correct handling of child-directed audiences, and consent where required.
  • Account for ad SDKs in your Data safety form and privacy policy, and measure their startup and size cost.

In-app purchases

For digital goods and features consumed in the app, Google Play's Payments policy generally requires Google Play Billing (with specific exceptions and regional programmes such as user choice billing in some countries). Physical goods and real-world services, such as food delivery or rides, use other payment methods. Check the current policy for your category and region.

Implementation details are covered in Subscription Billing in Android Apps.

Considerations for Indian audiences

  • Price sensitivity is high, so regional pricing and smaller price points (weekly plans, low-cost unlocks) can matter.
  • Many users are on entry-level devices, so heavy ad SDKs hurt performance more.
  • UPI and local payment methods are available through Google Play Billing in supported cases. Check current Play payment options.

Metrics to track

  • Revenue per daily active user, conversion to paid, churn, and refund rate.
  • Retention before and after adding ads. Ads that reduce retention may cost more than they earn.
  • Ratings and reviews mentioning ads or pricing.

Examples

Wallpaper app

Frequent, light use: native and rewarded ads, plus a one-time "remove ads" purchase.

Notes app with sync

Free local notes, with a subscription for cloud sync and AI features (ongoing server costs).

Exam prep app

A free sample of tests, with subscriptions for full access during the exam season.

Frequently asked questions

How much can I earn from ads?

It varies enormously by country, category, format and engagement. Estimate from your own test data rather than generic averages.

Can I accept payments via my own gateway?

For digital goods inside the app, Play's Payments policy generally requires Play Billing, with defined exceptions and regional programmes. Physical goods and services can use other payment methods.

Should a new app monetise from day one?

Often it's better to validate retention first, while designing the monetisation model early so you don't surprise users later.

Conclusion

Match monetisation to usage frequency and perceived value: light ads for frequent, low-value use, one-time purchases for clear upgrades, subscriptions for ongoing value, or a hybrid. Respect policies and UX, and measure the effect on retention.
