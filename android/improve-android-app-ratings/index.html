How to Improve Android App Ratings and Reviews

Ratings influence installs, but you can't buy or trick your way to a good rating without risking policy violations. The sustainable path is fixing what makes users unhappy, asking happy users at the right moment, and responding well to feedback.

Read your one-star reviews and patterns appear: "crashes when I open the camera", "lost my data after the update", "too many ads", "can't log in". Most low ratings come from a few fixable problems. Fixing those does more than any prompt strategy.

What drives ratings

1. Fix what reviews and vitals tell you

  • Filter reviews by keyword (crash, login, ads, slow) and by app version, and turn patterns into tickets.
  • Cross-check with Android vitals and crash reports. See Analytics and Crash Reporting.
  • Prioritise data loss and crashes, then performance, then annoyances.

2. Ask at the right moment with the In-App Review API

  • Ask after positive moments, never on launch, during a task, or after an error.
  • Play enforces quotas, so the dialog may not appear. Don't build a UI that depends on it showing.
  • Don't ask "Do you like the app?" first and send only happy users to Play. Review gating like that is against Play policy.

3. Give unhappy users a better channel

Add an always-available "Send feedback" or "Report a problem" option in settings, with device info attached (with consent). Users who can reach you easily are less likely to vent in a one-star review, and you get actionable details.

4. Reply to reviews well

  • Reply quickly, personally and specifically. Avoid copy-paste templates.
  • Acknowledge the problem, give a workaround if one exists, and say when a fix ships.
  • After fixing, reply again. Users can update their rating.

What not to do

  • Offer incentives for ratings, or buy reviews: prohibited, and risky for your account.
  • Gate review prompts by sentiment.
  • Ask repeatedly or in disruptive moments.

Examples

Utility app with crash complaints

Fix the top three crash clusters, then add review prompts after successful tasks. Rating improves over subsequent releases.

Ad complaints

Remove interstitials on launch, use rewarded ads, add a remove-ads purchase.

Login issues

Add Credential Manager, better error messages and an in-app help link.

Frequently asked questions

How fast do ratings change?

Play's displayed rating weights recent reviews, so improvements can show within weeks or months as new reviews arrive.

Can I remove unfair reviews?

You can flag reviews that violate Play's review policies. Ordinary negative reviews can't be removed. Reply and fix the issue instead.

Should I prompt for reviews in a beta?

Use direct feedback channels for testers. Review prompts are for production users.

Conclusion

Better ratings come from fixing crashes, data loss and annoyances, asking satisfied users at the right moment with the In-App Review API, offering an easy feedback channel, and replying helpfully, all within Play policy.
