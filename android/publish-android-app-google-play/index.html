🚀 App Publishing, Monetization, and Product Development

How to Publish an Android App on Google Play

Publishing is where many first apps stall: account verification, testing requirements, signing, policy forms and store listing details. This guide walks through the whole process in order, with the requirements current at the time of writing.

Your app works on your phone. Getting it onto Google Play involves a developer account, a signed release, a testing period, a store listing and several policy declarations. None of it is hard, but each step has details that can delay you by days if you miss them.

Requirements change

Play policies and requirements are updated regularly. At the time of writing, new apps and updates must target Android 16 (API level 36) from August 31, 2026, and new personal developer accounts must complete a closed test before applying for production access. Always confirm current rules in Play Console Help.

The path to production

1. Create a developer account

  • Register in the Play Console. There's a one-time registration fee.
  • Choose personal or organisation account. Organisations need verification details such as a D-U-N-S number.
  • Complete identity verification and contact-detail verification.

2. Prepare a signed release

  1. Set a unique applicationId, which is permanent, plus versionCode and versionName.
  2. Target the required API level, and test behaviour changes for that Android version.
  3. Enable R8 and resource shrinking, and test the minified build. See Prepare for Production.
  4. Generate an upload key and enrol in Play App Signing. See App Signing.
  5. Build an Android App Bundle: ./gradlew bundleRelease.

3. Store listing and policy forms

App name, short and full descriptionAccurate, no keyword stuffing. See Listing Optimization

Target audience and ads declarationExtra requirements if children are a target audience

4. Testing tracks

  • Internal testing: up to a limited number of testers, available within minutes. Use it for quick team checks.
  • Closed testing: invited testers (email lists or Google Groups). New personal developer accounts must run a closed test that meets Google's minimum tester count and duration before they can apply for production. Commonly cited requirements are 12 opted-in testers for 14 consecutive days. Check the current numbers in Play Console Help.
  • Open testing: public beta on your store listing.
Make the closed test useful

Recruit real testers (friends, colleagues, a community), ask them to use the app regularly, and collect feedback. Google expects genuine testing, and you'll find real bugs.

5. Release to production

  1. Apply for production access (for new personal accounts) after meeting testing requirements, answering questions about your testing.
  2. Create a production release, upload the AAB, and write release notes.
  3. Use a staged rollout (for example 10% → 50% → 100%) and watch crashes and ANRs in Android vitals.
  4. Review can take from hours to several days, longer for new accounts or sensitive permissions.

Situations

Student's first app

Personal account: plan two to three weeks for verification and closed testing before production.

Company app

Organisation account with team access, and internal testing for QA builds from CI.

Client project (agency)

Publish under the client's account, with you added as a user with limited permissions.

Common reasons for rejection or delays

  • Data safety form doesn't match the app or SDK behaviour.
  • Missing or inaccessible privacy policy, or no test credentials for login-gated apps.
  • Restricted permissions without justification (SMS, call log, background location).
  • Misleading metadata or impersonation in the listing.
  • Target API level below the requirement.

Frequently asked questions

Can I change my package name later?

No. The applicationId identifies the app on Play forever. A new ID means a new app listing.

How long does review take?

It varies, typically hours to a few days. New accounts and sensitive features can take longer.

Do I need an organisation account to publish?

No, but organisation accounts suit businesses and avoid the personal-account testing requirement. They require business verification.

Conclusion

Publishing on Google Play means a verified account, a signed App Bundle targeting the required API level, a complete listing and policy forms, a genuine closed test (for new personal accounts), and a staged production rollout. Start the slow steps early.
