Privacy, accessibility and reliability share a pattern: small decisions during development add up to a trustworthy app, and fixing them late is expensive. Build them into your definition of done.

Privacy by design

  • Ask for less: use the photo picker instead of storage permissions, approximate location when precise isn't needed, and system intents (camera, contacts picker) where possible.
  • Ask in context: request permissions when the feature is used, with a clear rationale. See Permissions and Privacy.
  • Collect and keep less: define retention and delete data you don't need.
  • Be transparent: an accurate Play Data safety form and privacy policy, matching what SDKs collect too.
  • Protect what you store: see Encrypted Storage.

  • Touch targets at least 48×48dp (Material components handle this).
  • Font scaling: use sp and test at the largest sizes (Android 14+ supports up to 200%). Avoid fixed heights that clip text.
  • Contrast: meet WCAG contrast ratios. Don't rely on colour alone.
  • Motion: respect the system "remove animations" setting.

Testing accessibility

  • Navigate your key flows with TalkBack and with Switch Access or a keyboard.
  • Run Accessibility Scanner on screens.
  • Compose UI tests can enable accessibility checks, and tests that find nodes by semantics help keep descriptions in place. See Compose UI Testing.
  • Use the Play Console pre-launch report, which flags accessibility issues.

Definition-of-done checklist

  1. Only necessary permissions, requested in context. Data safety form updated.
  2. TalkBack can complete the flow, and targets and contrast pass Accessibility Scanner.
  3. Works at the largest font size and in dark mode.
  4. Loading, empty, error and offline states handled.
  5. No main-thread I/O. Survives rotation and process death.

Frequently asked questions

Is accessibility legally required?

In many regions, accessibility laws apply to certain apps and organisations (for example the European Accessibility Act). Check what applies to you. Regardless, it widens your audience.

What crash-free rate is good?

Aim to stay well within Android vitals thresholds, and treat any regression as a release blocker. Many teams target very high crash-free user rates.

Conclusion

Trustworthy apps collect less and explain more, are usable with assistive technologies and large fonts, and don't crash or freeze. Put all three into your definition of done and test them like features.
