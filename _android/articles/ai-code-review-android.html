Code review exists to catch defects, share knowledge and keep the codebase coherent. AI review helps with the first goal, partly, and does little for the other two. Treat it as an extra reviewer with specific strengths, not a replacement for people.

What AI review is good and bad at

Usually good atUsually weak at
Common bug patterns: nullability, resource leaks, swallowed exceptionsBusiness rules and product intent
Android pitfalls: Context leaks, main-thread I/O, lifecycle misuseCross-module architecture decisions and history
Missing tests or obvious untested branchesWhether tests assert the right behaviour
Readability suggestions, namingKnowing which conventions your team actually follows (without rules)
Security smells: hard-coded secrets, logging tokensThreat modelling of a new feature

A focused review prompt

Review this diff for an Android app (Kotlin, Compose, Hilt, coroutines). Focus only on: 1) correctness bugs, 2) lifecycle/threading issues (main-thread I/O, leaked scopes/contexts), 3) security/privacy (secrets, logging personal data, exported components), 4) missing or weak tests for changed logic. For each finding: file:line, severity (high/medium/low), why it's a problem, minimal fix. Do not comment on formatting or style covered by ktlint. If unsure, say so.

Handling false positives

  • Require evidence (file and line, reasoning). Dismiss findings that can't point to a concrete problem.
  • Tune with rules: tell the reviewer about intentional patterns ("we use !! only after checkNotNull in tests").
  • Don't let AI comments block merges automatically. Humans decide.

Privacy and policy

Sending diffs to an AI service shares source code. Use tools and tiers approved by your organisation, exclude sensitive paths, and check data retention terms, especially for client projects under NDA.

What humans should still focus on

  • Does this change solve the right problem, as the product needs it?
  • Does it fit the architecture and future direction?
  • Is it understandable to the next person, and did the author learn something?
  • Are tests protecting meaningful behaviour?

Good uses

Self-review before opening a PR

The author runs AI review locally and fixes obvious issues before teammates spend time.

Large mechanical PRs

AI scans for inconsistencies in big migrations, and humans sample-review deeply.

Junior mentoring

AI explains "why" for common issues, and seniors focus on design feedback.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI replace a second human reviewer?

For low-risk changes some teams accept AI plus one human, but for most production code keep human review. AI misses intent and context.

Does AI review conflict with lint?

It shouldn't duplicate lint. Configure it to skip what lint and formatters already enforce.

How do I measure value?

Track accepted versus dismissed findings and bugs caught before merge, and adjust prompts or turn the reviewer off for low-value categories.

Conclusion

AI code review is a useful additional layer: good at common bugs, Android pitfalls and missing tests, weak at intent and architecture. Run deterministic checks first, prompt AI for evidence-backed findings, treat its output as suggestions, and keep humans responsible for design and knowledge sharing.
