"Skilled in Android, Kotlin, Java, Firebase, MVVM, Retrofit, Room..." appears on thousands of resumes, and proves nothing. Specific outcomes and verifiable links ("Published an expense tracker on Google Play; Room + Compose; 4.6★ from early users") get attention.

Resume structure

Writing impact bullets

Use: action + what + how + result.

WeakStrong
Worked on the app's UIRebuilt the checkout flow in Jetpack Compose with a single-state ViewModel, cutting UI code by about a third and fixing three recurring crash clusters
Used Room and RetrofitImplemented offline-first sync (Room as source of truth + WorkManager), so field agents could submit reports without connectivity
Improved performanceReduced cold start on a mid-range device by deferring SDK initialisation and adding a Baseline Profile (measured with Macrobenchmark)

Use real numbers you can explain in an interview. Never inflate them.

Skills section

List only what you can discuss confidently. Interviewers will ask.

Portfolio that proves skills

  • 2–3 strong projects beat ten tutorial clones. See project ideas in How to Become an Android Developer.
  • Each repo has a README: screenshots or a GIF, features, architecture summary, tech stack, how to run it, and trade-offs and next steps.
  • Clean history: meaningful commits over time, tests and CI badges.
  • A Play Store link if possible: shipping is strong evidence. See Launching as a Solo Developer.
  • A short case study (blog post) for your best project: problem, decisions and lessons learned.

Notes for Indian job markets

  • Many companies scan for specific stacks, so mirror the job description's terms honestly.
  • For freshers, internships, industrial training projects and published apps carry weight. Describe your role in team projects precisely.
  • Keep contact details professional, and add a LinkedIn profile consistent with the resume.

Common mistakes

  • Long skill lists with ratings ("Kotlin 9/10").
  • Projects without links, or with links to empty or broken repos.
  • Tutorial projects presented as original work.
  • Multi-page resumes for junior roles, or typos and inconsistent formatting.

Frequently asked questions

Should I include a photo?

Norms vary by country and company. In many international applications photos are discouraged. Follow local norms and the employer's instructions.

Are certificates useful?

They can help early on, but projects and shipped apps are stronger evidence. List certificates briefly.

Can I include AI-assisted projects?

Yes, if you understand and can explain every part. Interviewers will ask about decisions and code.

Conclusion

A strong Android resume shows evidence: impact bullets with honest numbers, a focused skills list, and links to well-documented projects and shipped apps. Make the proof one click away.
